UK take-home pay calculator 2026/27

Work out your salary after Income Tax, National Insurance, pension and student loan, using the 2026/27 rates for England, Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland.

Calculated on your device. Figures are rounded to the nearest penny.

2026/27 rates used

  • Personal Allowance: £12,570. It's reduced by £1 for every £2 earned over £100,000.
  • England, Wales and NI: 20% up to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140, and 45% above.
  • Scotland: 19%, 20%, 21%, 42%, 45% and 48% bands (starter rate up to £16,537, higher rate from £43,663, top rate over £125,140).
  • National Insurance (employees): 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above.
  • Student loans: 9% above £26,900 (Plan 1), £29,385 (Plan 2), £33,795 (Plan 4) or £25,000 (Plan 5). Postgraduate loans are 6% above £21,000.

Rates were checked against GOV.UK in November 2026. This is an estimate for a standard tax code (1257L), and your payslip may differ slightly because of your tax code, benefits or pay timing.

Questions

How much is £30,000 after tax?

In England, Wales or NI with no pension or student loan, it's about £25,120 a year (roughly £2,093 a month) in 2026/27. Enter your own figures above for an exact estimate.

What's the difference between net pay and salary sacrifice?

Both reduce your Income Tax. Salary sacrifice also reduces your National Insurance, so you take home a little more for the same pension contribution.

Does this include employer pension contributions?

No. It only shows what's deducted from your pay. Your employer's contribution is extra.
