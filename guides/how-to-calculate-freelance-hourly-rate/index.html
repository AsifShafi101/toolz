    How to work out your freelance hourly rate

    Updated 6 November 2026 · etoolbox guides

    The biggest mistake new freelancers make is taking their old salary and dividing it by 1,950 working hours. As a freelancer you pay your own holiday, sick days, pension and expenses, and you won't bill every hour you work. Here's a better way.

    Step 1: Decide what you want to earn

    Start with the yearly income you want before tax, the same as a salary. Then add what an employer would have paid on top: a typical workplace pension contribution is 3–5%, plus any training and kit they'd cover.

    Step 2: Add your business costs

    Add up a year of expenses: software, phone, insurance, accountant, equipment, travel, co-working and marketing. Many freelancers spend £2,000–£6,000 a year.

    Step 3: Work out your billable hours

    There are 52 weeks in a year. Take off holidays (say 5 weeks including bank holidays) and a week or two for illness and quiet spells, leaving about 45 weeks. Then be honest about how much of each week you actually bill: admin, sales and invoicing usually take 25–40% of your time. At 37.5 hours a week and 70% billable, that's about 1,180 billable hours a year.

    Step 4: Divide

    Hourly rate = (target income + costs) ÷ billable hours

    Example: (£45,000 + £4,000) ÷ 1,180 = £41.50 an hour, or about £310 a day. Compare that with £45,000 ÷ 1,950 = £23 an hour, which would leave you badly short.

    Then sanity-check it

    • Look at what others in your field and area charge. Rate surveys, job boards and freelancer communities help.
    • If you're VAT-registered, add VAT on top of your rate.
    • Review your rate every year. Your costs and experience go up.

    Once you've agreed work, send a professional quote and then an invoice.

    Questions

    What's a good day rate?

    It depends on your field and experience. Work out your minimum with the method above, then compare it with what others in your area charge.

    How many billable hours should I plan for?

    Many freelancers plan for around 1,100–1,400 billable hours a year once holidays, sickness and admin are taken out.

    Should I charge by the hour or by the project?

    Hourly is simplest at the start. As you get faster, fixed project prices usually earn you more for the same work.
