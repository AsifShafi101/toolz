      How to use the scientific calculator

      • Tap the keys or type straight into the screen with your keyboard. Press Enter or = for the answer.
      • SHIFT gives the second function shown above a key, e.g. SHIFT + x² for √, SHIFT + sin for sin⁻¹, SHIFT + log for 10ˣ.
      • S⇔D switches the answer between a fraction, a mixed number, a surd (like 2√2) and a decimal.
      • Choose DEG, RAD or GRAD for angles. Degrees is the default for GCSE.
      • Press an operator straight after an answer to keep going with Ans. Brackets you forget to close at the end are closed for you.
      • The table of values works out f(x) for a range of x, handy for plotting graphs.

      Functions included

      Fractions and decimals, x², x³, xʸ, √, ∛, x⁻¹, x!, logs (log, ln, 10ˣ, eˣ), sin, cos, tan and their inverses, π and e, nCr and nPr, percentages, standard form (×10ˣ), absolute value, memory (M+, M−, MR, MC) and the previous answer (Ans). These cover the GCSE and most A-level maths and science content.

      Good to know: online calculators can't be used in exams. For GCSE you'll need a physical calculator approved by your exam board. This is ideal for homework, revision and checking your working.

      Questions

      Is this the same as a Casio scientific calculator?

      It works in a similar way to the scientific calculators used at GCSE, such as the Casio fx-83GTCW and fx-85GTCW, with SHIFT functions, S⇔D, nCr and a table of values. It's an independent tool and isn't made by or affiliated with Casio.

      Can I type with my keyboard?

      Yes. Type numbers and symbols, use * for × and / for ÷, ^ for powers, and press Enter for the answer. You can also type words like sqrt, sin, log and pi.

      How do I get a fraction answer?

      Fraction answers are shown automatically when the result is an exact fraction. Press S⇔D to switch between fraction, mixed number, surd and decimal.

      Is it free?

      Yes. No sign-up and no adverts in the calculator. Your history is saved only in your own browser.
